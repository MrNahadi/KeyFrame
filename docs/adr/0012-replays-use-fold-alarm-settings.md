# 0012. Replays use each fold's own alarm settings, so the demo shows the evaluated alarms

Status: accepted
Decided-by: builder (stated: brief non-negotiable), 29 Sep 2026, while writing the paper (roadmap 15)
Question: The replay files applied one alarm setting to every run: the median of the four folds' chosen settings (45 s sustained at probability 0.7), the setting the all-loads export uses. The locked evaluation (`04_alarms.csv`) scores each held-out load with the setting chosen inside that fold's own training loads (40%: 300 s at 0.8; 60%: 60 s at 0.6; 75%: 30 s at 0.8; 85%: 30 s at 0.5). The demo therefore showed alarms on 9 of 13 fault runs, with different delays, where the evaluation reports 6 of 13; for example air cooler fouling at 40% load alarmed after 7 min 18 s in the demo and after 91 min 25 s in the evaluation. The median also draws on settings chosen with the replayed load's data in their training set, a small leak. Which should the demo show?
Decision: The evaluated alarms. `replay.fold_models` now gives each fold model `predict.fold_alarm_settings(fold)`, read from `04_alarms.csv`, and the replay files were regenerated. A test (`tests/test_replay_export.py`) holds every replay's alarm delay to the evaluated delay, allowing only the 10 s frame thinning. The locked scores are unchanged: this changes what the demo displays, not what was scored.
Basis: Brief ranked trade-off 1 (honest evaluation) and the non-negotiable that every number shown is measured on held-out loads; a demo that shows more alarms than the evaluation found overstates the model.
Consequences: The runs page and replays show 6 alarms on 13 fault runs, matching the model card. The runs page's start run is still the quickest alarm (air cooler fouling at 75% load). The all-loads export and the API keep the median setting, which the model card and paper describe as a deployment choice with no held-out score of its own.
